### Balance: calculation from the supplied rules

Assume “pays” means gross reward, with the four-shell cost charged on every trip.

| Route | Net outcomes | Expected net/trip | Expected net/minute | Net standard deviation |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Safe | +6 | 6 shells | 2 shells | 0 |
| Risky | +14 or −4, equally likely | 5 shells | 1.67 shells | 9 shells |

Risky expectation is `0.5 × 14 + 0.5 × (−4) = 5`. It offers **lower average income and greater uncertainty**, so P1’s preference is consistent with maximizing expected shells. Safe is not strictly dominant: risky can produce a larger realized gain and P3 reported enjoying that uncertainty.

The upgrade’s incremental payback is `20 ÷ 2 = 10 trips = 30 minutes`. That is break-even; additional trips make it profitable under these assumptions. Because the bonus is guaranteed on every trip, upgraded safe nets +8, and upgraded risky nets +16 or −2, averaging +7. The one-shell expected gap remains. Buying the upgrade must also leave enough shells to depart; starting wealth, recovery rules, and expected remaining playtime are unspecified.

### Next experiment

**First fix:** Label the currency “shells” and display cost, duration, probabilities, and resulting balance changes plainly. For example, risky should communicate “Spend 4 shells; 50% chance of +14 net, otherwise −4 net.” Preserve p04 as the baseline.

**Balance hypothesis:** Once comprehension is adequate, compare two versions with identical readable presentation: current risky payout 18 versus provisional payout **22**, with everything else held fixed. At 22, risky nets +18 or −4 and averages +7, trading better average income for less certainty. This is a test value, not an established optimum.

A practical six-hour allocation:

- **2 hours:** Readability changes, one payout toggle, and checks of displayed arithmetic and outcomes.
- **30 minutes:** Prepare neutral session tasks and recording notes.
- **2½ hours:** Short sessions with approximately four to six available target players, including novices and experienced players; record recruitment and prior exposure.
- **1 hour:** Compare observations and record the next decision.

Counterbalance variant order where possible. Observe comprehension without coaching, then record choices, reasons, available shells, outcomes, and assistance. Ask “What were you deciding between?” Avoid treating an even safe/risky split as success. If comprehension still blocks play, prioritize that finding and do not interpret confused choices as balance preferences.

### Revisit conditions

Keep changes when players can predict the consequences unaided and explain an intentional reason for their choice; look for whether circumstances or preferences give both routes a useful role. Ambiguous or conflicting results call for another small revision.

Even a promising next round would justify a small representative content slice before 50 cards. Expansion also requires evidence that additional cards create meaningful decisions and can be authored, integrated, and tested within the team’s capacity. Neither is established by the supplied p04 observations.
